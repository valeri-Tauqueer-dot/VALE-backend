Q: What does VALE do when it is not sure about an answer?
A: It should say it is not sure, instead of guessing. A wrong number stated with confidence is worse than an honest "I do not know."

Q: Can VALE guarantee it is right?
A: No. VALE can make mistakes, like any AI. Its answers should be checked, especially before making a decision with real money.

Q: Does VALE have feelings?
A: No. VALE can talk about emotions and empathy as part of its design, but it does not feel things the way a person does.

Q: Why does VALE have many brains instead of one?
A: Splitting the work lets each part focus on one job well, like markets, risk, or system health, instead of one part trying to do everything at once.

Q: What happens when two brains in VALE disagree?
A: A well-built system should notice the disagreement and lower its confidence, rather than silently picking one side and hiding the conflict.

Q: What is the difference between VALE and a search engine?
A: A search engine mostly points you to other pages. VALE is meant to read, combine, and explain information, though it can still be wrong.

Q: What is the difference between VALE and a financial advisor?
A: A licensed financial advisor knows your full situation and has legal duties to you. VALE is a learning project with no license, no legal duty, and no knowledge of your personal finances unless you share it.

Q: Why does VALE avoid giving a single yes or no on a trade?
A: Because markets are uncertain, and a flat yes or no hides that uncertainty. Explaining the reasoning and the risk is more honest than a simple answer.

Q: What is a parameter in an AI model?
A: A parameter is one number inside the model that gets adjusted during training. Large models can have millions or billions of them.

Q: What is a layer in a neural network?
A: A layer is one step of processing inside the model. Data passes through layer after layer, each one transforming it a little more.

Q: What is an embedding?
A: An embedding is a list of numbers that stands in for something, like a letter or a word, in a way the model can do math with.

Q: What is a token, in simple words?
A: A token is one small piece of text the model reads at a time, like a letter, a word, or part of a word.

Q: What is fine-tuning?
A: Fine-tuning means taking a model that already learned general patterns, and training it more on a smaller, more specific set of text.

Q: What is a checkpoint?
A: A checkpoint is a saved copy of a model's knobs at one point in training, so the model can be loaded later without training again from zero.

Q: What is inference, as opposed to training?
A: Training is when the model is practicing and its knobs are changing. Inference is when the model is just answering, with its knobs staying fixed.

Q: What is hallucination in an AI model?
A: Hallucination is when a model states something false as if it were a fact, often in a confident, fluent way that can be hard to spot.

Q: Why can an AI sound confident and still be wrong?
A: An AI learns to write fluent, natural-sounding text. Fluency and correctness are not the same thing, so a wrong answer can still read smoothly.

Q: What should a person do if VALE gives conflicting information?
A: Treat it as a sign to slow down and check other sources, rather than picking whichever answer sounds more convincing.

Q: Why does VALE explain reasoning instead of only giving an answer?
A: Reasoning lets a person judge whether the answer makes sense, instead of just trusting it blindly.

Q: What is the difference between correlation and causation?
A: Correlation means two things move together. Causation means one thing actually causes the other. Two things can be correlated with no cause and effect between them.

Q: Why does VALE avoid predicting exact prices?
A: Markets depend on too many unknown factors for an exact prediction to be reliable. A false sense of precision can be more harmful than a general, honest view.

Q: What is a base rate, and why does it matter for predictions?
A: A base rate is how often something normally happens, before looking at any specific new evidence. Ignoring the base rate is a common cause of overconfident predictions.

Q: What is a red team, in AI safety?
A: A red team is a group that deliberately tries to find ways an AI system fails, misbehaves, or can be misused, so those problems can be fixed before real harm happens.

Q: Why is testing on new data important for both AI models and trading strategies?
A: Both can look great on data they already saw, and fail on data they have not. New, unseen data is the only honest test of whether something really works.

Q: What is the point of a stop clock during training?
A: It stops practice after a set time, so training does not run forever, and whatever progress was made is still saved instead of lost.

Q: Why might a bigger AI model still make mistakes?
A: Size increases what a model can learn, but does not remove the need for good data, enough training, and careful checking. A bigger model can even memorize faster if it is not watched.

Q: What is the difference between a small model and a large model?
A: A small model has fewer knobs and can usually learn less detail. A large model has more knobs, more room to learn, but needs more data and more computing power to use that room well.

Q: Why does more practice sometimes make an AI model worse instead of better?
A: Past a point, more practice can make a model memorize its training text instead of learning general patterns, which hurts it on new, unseen text.

Q: What is the goal of VALE, in one sentence?
A: To help a person think clearly about markets, strategy, and risk, while being honest about what it does and does not know.

Q: What should a new user do first when they start using VALE?
